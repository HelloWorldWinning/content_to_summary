### PART 1. Author's Map（作者论证地图）

#### 1. Core Thesis（核心论点）
Adobe 预谋并实施了从“买断制所有权”向“租用制订阅模式”的转变，虽然短期内榨取了创纪录的利润，但其对用户的掠夺性策略（隐藏退出费、暗黑模式、强制 AI 赋税、滥用版权条款）摧毁了品牌信任；而在生成式 AI 削弱其专业技能壁垒、新竞争者崛起的当下，这种租租资本主义模型正引发市场对其未来生存能力的根本怀疑，导致其股价在营收创新高的同时发生灾难性崩盘。

#### 2. Evidentiary Base（证据库）
*   **硬数据（Hard Data）**：
    *   财务数据：2025 年创纪录的 238 亿美元营收（旧买断制最后一年仅约 40 亿美元）；股价从最高点约 700 美元跌至 2026 年春季的约 200 美元（跌幅约 65%）。
    *   价格对比：旧版 Master Collection 买断价 $2,600；现订阅全家桶高达 $70/月（$840/年），3 年订阅费即抵过去终身买断价，40 年职业生涯租金达 $33,000 至 $100,000。
    *   监管与法律裁决：2024 年美国司法部（DOJ）和 FTC 起诉 Adobe 隐藏费用与取消陷阱，Adobe 支付 1.5 亿美元和解金；2023 年因美、英、欧监管机构干预，Adobe 放弃 200 亿美元收购 Figma 并支付 10 亿美元违约金。
*   **专家证言与理论框架（Expert Testimony / Theoretical Citations）**：
    *   Yanis Varoufakis（希腊经济学家）：提出“技术封建主义”（Techno-Feudalism），指责大科技公司占据数字土地，用户退化为缴纳地租的农奴。
    *   Slavoj Žižek（哲学家）：论述数字资本主义是对“公共资源”（Commons）的新型私有化。
    *   Brett Christophers（政治经济学家）：提出“食租者资本主义”（Rentier Capitalism），企业不靠产品创新盈利，而是靠控制资产产权抽取租金。
    *   世界经济论坛（WEF）达沃斯论坛：经济学家对食租资本主义（收益超过生产回报）的定义。
*   **历史先例（Historical Precedent）**：
    *   反竞争收购与抹杀：1994 年收购 Freehand 被 FTC 阻拦后等待 10 年禁令过期；2005 年收购 Macromedia（$34亿）并永久关闭 Freehand，同时掌控 Flash 和 Dreamweaver。
*   **案例与现象（Anecdote & Cases）**：
    *   暗黑模式（Dark Patterns）：将“Annual Paid Monthly”预选为默认方案，解约需支付剩余合同 50% 的违约金；YouTube 上大量“如何取消 Adobe 订阅”教程。
    *   AI 策略与条款反弹：摄影计划从 $9.99/月涨至 $14.99/月（嵌入 Firefly AI）；2024 年更新服务条款宣称可访问用户作品进行人工与自动审查，引爆社区抵制。
    *   竞争威胁：Canva 收购 Affinity（提供买断/免费替代）；Blackmagic 的 DaVinci Resolve 在视频剪辑领域分流 Premiere 用户。

#### 3. Logical Warrant（逻辑桥梁/推理路径）
*   **三段论结构**：
    *   *大前提*：依赖控制资产而非产品质量来抽取“地租”的食租企业，必然会导致产品质量衰退、用户怨声载道，并在遇到无法被兼并的毁灭性新技术/竞争者时瞬间丧失护城河。
    *   *小前提*：Adobe 通过取消买断制、实施垄断收购与暗黑取消陷阱建立了食租封建帝国；但生成式 AI 正在消解其“专业工具难度”这一核心壁垒，同时 regulators 阻止了其收购 Figma，新替代品不断涌现。
    *   *结论*：尽管 Adobe 目前依赖滞后的订阅惯性实现创纪录营收，但资本市场已预见其基于垄断和强制锁定的食租模式的破产，导致其股价剧烈崩盘。

#### 4. Stated Scope（适用范围与界限）
*   作者明确指出，Adobe 并非孤例，而是整个科技与现代商业（Spotify、Amazon 删除 Kindle 电子书、监控型车险、住房租凭化、订阅制普及）向“技术封建主义”和“无所有权时代”演变的发源地与缩影。

#### 5. Implicit Opponent（隐性对立面）
*   对立观点：华尔街与科技巨头管理层所宣扬的“订阅制是双赢”叙事——即“订阅制降低了用户的门槛，使软件能够获得持续的云端更新与智能化升级，企业获得的经常性收入（ARR）代表了健康的客户忠诚度”。

#### 6. Intended Takeaway（预期效果/行动指引）
*   警示读者认清现代科技资本通过“租用代替所有”剥夺个人资产与自由的本质；呼吁创作者拥抱 Affinity、DaVinci 等替代方案，打破对垄断食租者的路径依赖。

### PART 2. Hostile-But-Very-Fair REVIEW（冷酷且公平的同行审阅）

#### 1. Steelman（最强版本重构）
Adobe 展现了一种极端的资本抽取范式：它利用历史上建立的行业标准地位，强行将买断制转为订阅制，构建了极高的资产壁垒与极高昂的转换成本。这种模式在短期内创造了惊人的现金流，但由于其盈利不再建立在“赢得客户”而是“困住客户”上，公司不可避免地陷入产品质量恶化（Enshitification）与掠夺性暗黑模式。当监管机构封锁其通过收购消除竞争（如 Figma）的退路，且生成式 AI 从根本上降低了创作门槛时，市场意识到其高昂的订阅费缺乏长期可持续性，从而对其股票进行了剧烈的去泡沫化惩罚。

#### 2. Weakest Link（最薄弱环节）
*   **核心脆弱断言**：
    > *"For Adobe, AI is the opposite, it's the angry guy kicking the door in... Firefly lets people generate images from Adobe's stock images instead of buying them... By plugging the AI like mad, they effectively contributed to their own redundancy."*（对 Adobe 而言，AI 是破门而入的敌人……它让用户自食其力不再购买素材，Adobe 疯狂推进 AI 实际上是在促成自己的淘汰。）
*   **失效逻辑**：作者将生成式 AI 简化为“杀死 Adobe 的凶手”，忽略了企业级市场的核心诉求。Adobe 推进 Firefly 的核心商业护城河并非仅仅是生成图像，而是**“商业无忧的版权担保”（Legal Indemnification）**。大型企业（Fortune 500）不敢轻易使用 Midjourney 或 Stable Diffusion，因为存在巨大的版权诉讼风险，而 Adobe 使用自有合法版权库训练的 Firefly 正是企业级客户的刚需。如果企业客户因为法律合规性而更深度地绑定在 Adobe AI 生态中，作者所谓的“AI 摧毁 Adobe 商业模式”这一关键论点就会崩塌。

#### 3. Evidence Audit（证据审计：选择性忽略与偏差）
*   **股价下跌的因果倒置与归因偏误**：文本将 Adobe 股价从 2021 年高点下跌 65%（至 2026 年）归咎于“市场厌恶其道德败坏和 AI 自残”。但作者隐瞒了宏观环境：2021 年底是全球科技股在零利率泡沫下的历史绝对高点，随后全行业 SaaS（软件即服务）板块在加息周期中均经历了 40%-70% 的估值回归。将整个 SaaS 估值模型的大调整纯粹归因于用户对 Adobe 订阅制的反感，存在严重的归因偏差。
*   **遗漏关键企业级数据（Enterprise B2B）**：文本完全聚焦于个人创作者、自由职业者（Freelancers）的体验（如 $70/月太贵、暗黑取消陷阱）。但 Adobe 营收的大头来自于企业级批量授权（Enterprise License Agreement）。企业 IT 部门极其喜欢订阅制的可预测预算与集中化管理，个人用户的取消率（Churn Rate）并不能直接推导出公司整体基本面的衰亡。

#### 4. Hidden Premises（未明示前提）
*   **假设一**：买断制在云计算时代依然具备财务与技术可行性。
    *   *必须提出的质疑*：云端协同、跨设备同步、服务器端 GPU 渲染和持续的网络安全防护都需要巨大的持续算力与运维成本。如果继续实行 2005 年的单机买断制，软件公司如何支付高昂的云端基础设施账单？
*   **假设二**：创作者可以轻松迁移离开 Adobe 生态。
    *   *必须提出的质疑*：尽管 Affinity 和 DaVinci 很出色，但印刷厂的色准标准（Pantone）、出版业的 .INDD 格式、影视工业的管线集成（Pipeline Integration）依然被 Adobe 垄断。个人抛弃 Adobe 容易，但整个工业供应链的转换成本极高，这是否意味着 Adobe 的食租能力比作者想象的要坚固得多？

#### 5. The Blind Side（盲区与另一种视角）
*   **技术演进的必然成本**：文本将订阅制完全等同于“道德败坏的食租”。但从软件工程视角看，买断制会导致极其恶劣的“隔代升级困难”——软件厂商不得不隐藏重大功能直到大版本发布，且必须维持对几十个旧版本的兼容。订阅制在客观上确实实现了功能的即时交付和软件生态的持续迭代。
*   **Figma 失败的另一面**：收购 Figma 失败虽然让 Adobe 损失了 10 亿美元违约金，但也使其保留了 190 亿美元现金。Figma 独立上市后同样需要面对增长压力和货币化难题，竞争并非单向的击垮，而是双向的消耗战。

#### 6. One Hit Kill（致命一击）
> **“如果 Adobe 股价的下跌确实源于用户对其食租模式的抵制和 AI 对其业务的侵蚀，而非宏观 SaaS 估值回调；那么为何在文本所指责的‘暴政’最严重的 2023-2025 年期间，Adobe 的企业级净续约率（NDR）与订阅收入依然连续创下历史新高（达 238 亿美元）？如果最了解其产品价值的企业客户用真金白银选择了留存，你如何证明其商业模式正处于‘死循环’而非仅仅是散户的情绪反弹？”**

#### 7. What Would Settle It（判定性证据）
*   **验证作者正确**：未来 8-12 个季度内，Adobe 的净迟滞率（Net Revenue Retention, NRR）跌破 100%，企业级订阅流失率显著上升，且 Canva/Affinity 在 B2B 工业级标准市场占据 30% 以上份额。
*   **验证作者错误**：Adobe 通过 Firefly 企业版实现 AI 业务变现（ARR 显著增加），其经常性收入继续保持 10%+ 的同比复合增长，股价随降息周期与 AI 收益兑现重新创下历史新高。

Adobe products used to feel like a safe pair of hands that always worked. You'd really struggle to complain with anything, but now they're just something else entirely and everyone's sick of them. The software you used to buy once and own forever, you now rent by month at a price that climbs every year. We lost the tours we could trust and own, and in exchange we got rising subscriptions, mandatory AI nobody asked for, and a fee for the privilege of leaving. But Adobe is finally getting what they deserve. Caught in a death spiral where all of their greed and cynical corporate strategy are catching up with them. It's actually been their best year ever, a record 23.8 billion dollars in revenue. So then why have their stocks crashed magnificently by around 65% from their peak, which goes against the entire tech market boom right now, even with record profits, the market hates them. Well, this is because there's something much stranger going on with the company that redefined creativity for a generation. It's all just the end point of a process they've been orchestrating deliberately for years. You see, Adobe used to be one of those companies, whose products had everyone creating tutorials and how to do cool things in Photoshop, Illustrator or After Effects. But now, you'll just find hundreds of tutorials and how to cancel those subscriptions. It looks easy, but it isn't. That's why these videos exist. Here's how the trap worked. When you signed up, Adobe stood you towards a plan called Annual Paid Monthly. It was pre-selected for you, and it looks like a simple month to month deal. But it wasn't. What you'd agree to was a year long contract, and if you tried to leave before that year was up, Adobe charged you an early termination fee, while half of everything you had left to pay. On a basic plan that was a surprise bill of hundreds of dollars, just for the crime of wanting to stop. And the best part, that fee was hidden in fine print, tucked behind tiny icons you had to hover over to even know it existed. Then there was the cancellation itself. It doesn't just click a button. You got funneled through page after page of menus, discounts and prompts. Every step was built to weigh you down until you just gave up and kept on paying. The kind of thing, the industry politely calls a dark pattern, meaning a user interface designed to trick you into doing things. If you look on those how to cancel Adobe tutorials, you'll find thousands of comments from those who aren't able to, maybe because of an error or a missing button. It got so horrendous that in 2024 the government sued Adobe. The Department of Justice and the FTC accused them of trapping customers with hidden fees and a deliberately painful cancellation process, and they named two executives personally. So Adobe finally paid up a $150 million settlement, or while denying any wrongdoing. But the other story is that users had something to cancel in the first place, and why? Until this, Adobe had honoured one simple rule. You bought the software and it was yours. The full creative suite wasn't cheap, but you owned it. You could use it for a decade, and you only paid again when a new version was truly worth it. Then in 2013 Adobe killed that rule. It announced that Creative Suite 6 would be the last version anyone could ever buy outright. From now on you didn't own Photoshop, you rented it every month forever, and the day you stopped paying was the day you lost the tours your career was built on. They gave it a warm, weightless name as if it were a gift, behold, the creative cloud. Overnight, a customer who paid once every few years became one who paid every single month. And once your life's work lived inside Adobe's files and formats, leaving meant incinerating all of it. The payoff was obscene. In its last year, selling box software, Adobe made around $4 billion. A decade later, it pulls in over $23 billion a year. And most people badly underestimate how much more they hand over by renting it. The old master collection cost around $2,600, but you paid once and it was yours. The full creative cloud suite today costs up to $70 a month, a little over $840 a year, which means every three years of renting now cost you what the entire box suite used to cost to own outright. Even on the kindest possible assumption that Adobe would never raise prices again, even though this is something they've never once managed, the full suite at around $840 a year comes to over $33,000 in rent before you retire. That single move became the most copy decision in modern business. Every executive in tech watched Adobe swap ownership for rent and get rich, then did the same to their own customers one industry at a time. The subscription world you live in now, where almost nothing is truly yours, was built in what Adobe proved in 2013. You might have heard of techno-futilism.
